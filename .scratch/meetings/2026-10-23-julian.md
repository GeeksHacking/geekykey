## 1. What we heard since 8 Oct

### 1.1 Our capstone teacher's feedback

Our capstone teacher raised these points with us, so we planned the showcase and checkpoint 7 with them in mind:

- GeekyKey is too generic. The token could be more fun, like DEFCON's latest badge with its lights and lasers.
- The stations could use a better screen, such as an OLED, and show more about the person who tapped, such as their capstone group.
- A game could run on a screen when someone taps.
- Everything we do so far could be done with a phone.
- He understands that we're building the product for GeeksHacking, and suggested we ask Julian what GeeksHacking wants from it (section 4).

What we take from it: a phone should be needed only once, to claim a token. During the event, people see what happens at the stations and on a screen that everyone nearby sees at the same time, which a phone can't do. Two of his ideas go into checkpoint 7: the station shows the visitor's name and progress after a tap (3.3), and a big screen reacts to taps (3.4).

On the token itself: NTAG213 tags have no battery, so they can't light up on their own. Tags with an LED that lights from the reader's field exist, but they need testing against our RC522 reader, whose field is weak. A 3D-printed shell with a few collectible designs is a cheaper way to make the token fun.

### 1.2 How the showcase day runs

From a student who exhibited at an earlier Capstone Showcase:

- **Two segments.** Judges assess the booths in the morning. After lunch comes the public segment, when parents, mentors and other visitors walk round the booths.
- **The layout.** Booths stand along the walls, in three areas divided by walls, of about 3, 6 and 5 booths. From inside one area you can't see the others. That was an earlier year's layout, so this year's needs confirming.

We also have **100 NTAG213 tags**. That's about one per family in the public segment, with some kept back for judges, demos and spares.

We expect many parents to come for their own child's booth, and the walls hide the other areas. The public segment is where visitors need a reason to go further, and that's what the showcase game is for.

## 2. The game we propose for the showcase

For the public segment:

- Partner stations stand at other groups' booths, at least one in each walled area, chosen from the station partners page once booths are assigned.
- A tap lights the station and plays a sound, and the station shows the visitor's name and progress, for example `Hi Alex` / `+10  2/3 areas`.
- A big screen at our booth shows a live map of the hall, with each station pulsing as taps arrive, and a leaderboard.
- Every 20 minutes a bonus moves to the quietest area, for example "Research booths x2 until 3:20", shown on the map and on the stations.

During judging, the stations run no game, or a short trail for judges if the teachers allow it. At our booth, a judge taps a token and watches the tap reach the big screen.

Why this game:

- A phone can't do it. The map, and the station's light and sound, are seen by everyone nearby at once.
- It measures something the showcase can test: how many walled areas each token reached, straight from the taps.
- It runs on checkpoint 7's base features (3.1, 3.3 and 3.4), with this event's own game and settings on top, so it tests both halves of the split in 3.2.

## 3. The plan for checkpoint 7

The spec's checkpoint 7 already has the organizers' station endpoints, the station board, passport settings, the tap log, the stats, the export, the QR fallback and the limit per user. The plan adds the following.

### 3.1 Timed games and timed rules

Each station can run a different game, and its game can change at set times. An organizer sets a schedule before the day. Each line of the schedule names:

- a game,
- the stations it runs on, some or all,
- a start and an end,
- the game's rules for that period, such as its points or a multiplier.

A tap is judged by the games scheduled on its station at the time of the tap. That's `tapped_at` when the station sent one, and otherwise when the tap arrived. So a tap saved during one period and sent during the next still counts under the first. With two games on one station, a tap still gets one status, as spec section 3.7 already says: `accepted` if any game counted it, otherwise `cooldown` if a game held it back, otherwise `no_game`.

On the day, the judging segment runs no game or a judges' trail, and the public segment runs the passport, with the bonus moving every 20 minutes.

Whether organizers can also change the schedule during the event is question 4.4.

### 3.2 The base, and one event's own

Every event that uses the Event Service gets the base. Each event adds its own games and settings.

| The base, for every event | One event's own |
|---|---|
| Stations: creating them, their keys, disabling them, the station board | Which games run, and their rules |
| Storing taps: the statuses, the cooldown, the QR fallback | The schedule of games and rules |
| Users, names, checking login links, organizers | What the stations and the big screen show, such as booth details and questions |
| The tap log, the stats, the export | The theme and branding |
| The passport (stamps and points) as the default game | Add-ons at a booth, such as a partner's own device |
| Running the schedule, and passing the station's text back | |

In `apps/event-service`, the line should be easy to see and checked by a tool:

- `src/event/` keeps the base.
- `src/games/` has one folder per game. Each game has the same interface: given a tap and its context, it returns a status, points and the station's text, and it keeps its own record of what it counted. The passport moves here first, out of `src/event/tap-pipeline.ts`, with no change in behaviour.
- `src/showcase/` holds this event's settings: which games run, the schedule, and the booth texts.
- A lint rule stops `src/event/` importing from `src/games/` or `src/showcase/`, so the base never depends on one event.

When the Event Service moves to its own repo after the showcase (BAC-39), the base and the game interface are what another event's Event Service can start from.

### 3.3 What the station shows after a tap

Today a tap's answer carries only a status (agreement 6.1), and the station picks its words from a fixed table. The plan adds an optional field with the station's text, such as two lines of up to 16 characters for today's screen. The Event Service fills it in its hand-off answer (11.2), and the GeekyKey Service passes it to the station unchanged, as it already does with statuses.

- Old stations keep working, because the firmware contract already tells a station to ignore fields it doesn't know.
- Each event decides what its stations say, which matches the split in 3.2.
- The station shows the booth's details, and the visitor's name and progress. It can't show a visitor's capstone group, because the Event Service doesn't have one, and most afternoon visitors are parents. A guest shows as "Guest".
- It changes sections 6.1 and 11.2 of the agreement, so it needs Ethan's approval, and firmware work from Zi Tao.

### 3.4 The big screen

One screen at our booth, built into the Event screens. It shows the hall map, the leaderboard, the current bonus and its countdown.

- Agreement section 12 puts leaderboards out of scope today. This brings one into scope, for the big screen only (backlog ticket BAC-62). It shows the names people chose. Anyone can change theirs, and guests show as "Guest".
- It's one screen rather than one per booth, because the screens' calls go through a Worker. One screen polling every 2 seconds for 6 hours makes about 10,800 requests, and the showcase day is already estimated at 60,000 to 90,000 of the free plan's 100,000 (question 4.5).

### 3.5 The look of the screens

A sci-fi look, in the style of games such as Wuthering Waves and Arknights: Endfield. Endfield's black and yellow is close to GeeksHacking's own colours. The rules that keep it useful:

- Animate only a change: a stamp earned, which is the one long animation; points counting up; a stamp slot filling; a station's online pulse; the bonus countdown. Never animate on a poll that changed nothing.
- Thin borders, corner brackets, monospace labels and a faint grid give it structure. Nothing that's only decoration moves.
- Use CSS and `tw-animate-css`, which both apps already have. No new animation library, and no motion when the device asks for reduced motion.
- Do the big screen first, since judges and crowds look at it. The phone passport needs less.

The screens are Ethan's, so this is a proposal for him.

### 3.6 How much fits

There are 19 days to the backend freeze on Wed 11 Nov. The rehearsal is on Sat 7 Nov, the A/B test at the grad tea on Thu 12 Nov, and the showcase on Wed 18 Nov.

- **Must.** Checkpoint 7 as the spec has it.
- **Should.** Timed games and rules, the station's text, and the big screen with the hall map and the leaderboard.
- **Could.** A bigger station screen, an OLED or a colour TFT, which is Zi Tao's call. Tags that light up. The full animation pass.
- **Stretch.** A light 3D model of the hall on the big screen, loaded only there, because a 3D library is too heavy for phones. A plugin system, where each game is packaged on its own, so another team can add one without changing the Event Service's code, and the settings choose which to load. Until then, games live in `src/games/` and the settings pick them.
- **Not before the showcase.** More than one event in one Event Service.

## 5. Guests at the showcase (Julian and Qing Guan)

Background is in ADR 0005 and BAC-54. In the public segment, many visitors' phones aren't signed in to Google. "Continue as guest" on the token page signs them in through Firebase's anonymous sign-in, with no account. The token page's button then opens the passport as the same user, through a login link that the GeekyKey Service signs.

### 5.1 Are guests OK?

ADR 0004, logging in through Firebase with Google or GeeksHacking's login, was their decision on 8 Oct. Guests add a third way to sign in.

- Firebase deletes anonymous accounts after 30 days when automatic clean-up is on, and doesn't bill for them.
- A login link is valid for 60 seconds and works once. Its key comes from the Event Service key, so a leaked link key can log someone in to our Event Service only.
- Guests are already built, and a guest's claim passed checkpoint 6's check on production. If they say no, the guest button comes off the token page, and the desk helps visitors sign in with Google instead.
